The New Electricity Tariff: What the State Charges and Pays per Unit

รู้ยังอัตราค่าไฟฟ้าใหม่ รัฐซื้อขายหน่วยละเท่าใด

Summary

  • He works through the new Metropolitan Electricity Authority household tariff for 350 units a month: the first 200 units at 3 baht (600 baht), the next 150 units at about 4.16 baht (623 baht), plus a 24.62-baht service charge, Ft of about 0.16 baht per unit and 7% VAT, for a bill of about 1,396 baht, or about 3.73 baht per unit before VAT.
  • He notes the government's one-million-rooftop programme supports 5 kW systems that produce about 550 units a month, with surplus power bought at 2.20 baht per unit, about 41% below his 3.73-baht average.
  • He contrasts Bangladesh, which buys power from rooftop solar with batteries at about 2.86 baht per unit while selling at about 2.21 baht, roughly 29% more than it charges, to cut LNG imports; at about 1,000 baht per million BTU, he says gas alone costs over 8 baht per unit (about 120 units per million BTU).
  • He calls the Thai approach unfair twice over: a low 2.20-baht buy price where he argues about 3.50 baht would be fair, and a 50,000-baht handout funded by a 200,000-million-baht loan that vulnerable households may struggle to reach among 26 million households. Note that the Bangladesh rate covers solar with battery storage, so it is not a like-for-like comparison with plain rooftop solar.

People's solar: Thailand's plan compared with India's

เปรียบเทียบโซลาร์ภาคประชาชน รัฐบาลอนุทิน VS รัฐบาลอินเดีย

Summary

  • Thailand's plan gives each house a flat grant of 50,000 baht, for a 5 kW rooftop system.
