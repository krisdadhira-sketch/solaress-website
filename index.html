FAQ

Straight answers.

Does Thailand give net metering to homeowners?

Not true net metering. Thailand uses net billing: the solar power you use yourself replaces grid power at the full retail price, and the surplus you export is bought at a fixed lower rate of 2.20 baht per unit.

How long is the payback for a home?

Typically about 4 to 7 years for an on-grid system when most solar is used in the daytime. Batteries lengthen payback but add backup and night-time use.

Is there a tax benefit?

Yes. A personal income-tax deduction of up to 200,000 baht applies to on-grid rooftop systems up to 10 kWp connected between 3 March 2026 and 31 December 2028.

Will my solar work during a blackout?

A standard on-grid inverter switches off for safety when the grid fails. You need a hybrid inverter with a battery and a backup circuit to keep essential loads running.

How much maintenance does it need?

Very little. Panels are usually cleaned once or twice a year, with one yearly inspection of wiring, protection devices and earthing.

Can the inverter run without a CT?

Yes for a plain on-grid inverter that is allowed to export. No for a hybrid inverter or any system that must limit export: the CT is the inverter's only way to see what the building takes from the grid.

Checklist

Before your assessment: what to send us

Planning solar for your home? Please send us the following so we can assess your house.

You can upload all of these directly in the consultation form above. Go to the form

  1. Location

    A Google Maps pin (GPS) of the house.

  2. Electricity use

    Your last 3–12 monthly electricity bills.

    A list of your electrical appliances, from the largest load to the smallest, with how many hours a day each is used and at what time of day (day or night).

  3. Roof or installation area

    Photos of where the solar panels would go.

    The approximate size of the area, and which way it faces.

    The roof type and material (metal sheet, tile, concrete, etc.).

    Anything that casts shade on it (trees, buildings).

  4. Main distribution board (MDB)

    A photo with the cover open, plus a photo of the wall space next to it where the inverter would be mounted.

  5. Cable route

    How cables can run from the roof to the MDB (conduit, ceiling space, outside wall), with photos if possible.

  6. Grid meter

    A photo of the meter, showing its rating label (for example 15(45) A) and whether it is single-phase or three-phase.

  7. Main cable

    The size of the cable from the meter to the MDB (sq.mm), and whether it is copper or aluminium.

  8. Single-line diagram

    The house's electrical diagram, if you have one.

  9. Close-up photos of equipment

    Sharp, close photos where we can read the details, for example:

    • the main breaker and other breakers: brand, rating (A), number of poles and breaking capacity (kA);
    • the printing on the cable jacket: size (sq.mm), type (for example THW, VCT, NYY), copper or aluminium;
    • the rating label on the meter, and on any existing inverter or pump.
  10. Point person

    Name, phone number and LINE ID of one person, such as your electrician or caretaker. We will:

    • ask them for any further information during the assessment;
    • work with them on site during installation;
    • contact them for future support (checks, maintenance, troubleshooting).
